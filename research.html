Detecting Invisible Groundwater Discharge Patterns Across Tumon Bay, Guam

Investigating vital habitats sustained by groundwater discharge

University of Guam × NOAASep 2023 – presentages 13–14

Tumon Bay has a secret: fresh groundwater quietly seeping up through the reef, invisible unless you know how to look. So I went looking — first hunting down the seeps across the marine preserve, then swimming photo transects to meet the corals living around them. Turns out the reef was into it: the freshwater helped, and pH lined up with which coral species showed up (R said so, significantly). And for the fair, I built the whole preserve in Blender and 3D-printed it.

  • part 1: find the freshwater seeps in the preserve
  • part 2: photo transects + visual surveys around them
  • stats: pH vs. coral species, modeled in R
  • a 3D-printed model of the preserve, lit and built in Blender

The Mystery Nudibranch

Identifying a nudibranch from the Marine Lab's tanks with molecular techniques

UOG Marine LaboratoryOct – Dec 2023age 15

IDENTIFIED

A nudibranch nobody could name showed up in the Marine Lab's tanks, and 15-year-old me was very sure it could be a brand-new species. So I went full forensics: flash-froze them in liquid nitrogen, ran them through solution after solution (literally), pipetted a truly unreasonable amount, and pulled out their DNA. We sent it offshore for sequencing, and the verdict came back… Phestilla lugubris. Not new to science — but officially ID'd.

Then the tanks lost control, the nudibranchs didn't make it, and the project went under with them. RIP, little guys.
